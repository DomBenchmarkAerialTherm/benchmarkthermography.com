Why scan

Water under a flat roof doesn't show from the ground.

On a low-slope commercial roof, a small membrane failure lets water into the insulation, where it stays. The surface looks fine. The roof loses its insulating value, the deck starts to corrode, and the problem grows until it shows up inside the building.

After sunset, wet insulation holds heat longer than dry insulation. A thermal camera flown over the roof sees those temperature differences as anomalies, and paired visual frames show exactly where each one is.

What you get

We map it. Your roofer verifies it.

The report marks each thermal anomaly on the roof plan with matched thermal and visual images, so your roofing contractor knows exactly where to core-verify. Whether an anomaly is wet insulation, a repair patch, or ponding gets confirmed by a core sample, not by us. And since we don’t sell repairs, we have no reason to find problems that aren’t there.

It also gives you a dated baseline. Repeat the scan and you can show a board, an insurer, or a buyer how the roof is actually trending, with pictures.

Insurers have started asking for this kind of roof documentation at renewal, and it is a dated record of the roof's condition if you ever have to file a claim.

How it works

Three steps. Nobody has to be on site.

1

Send an address

We scope the roof from satellite imagery, check the airspace, ask you a few questions about the site, and send you a fixed quote and a short access release to sign.

2

We fly after sunset

A liability insurance certificate arrives before the flight, with your organization named as additional insured on request. The flight is exterior only from above the roof. No roof access, no building entry, no one needs to meet us.

3

You get the report

Annotated roof plan, matched thermal and visual frames for every anomaly, and plain-language notes on what to have a roofer verify. Delivered as a PDF within days of the flight.

We report thermal anomalies; we don't diagnose roofs. We are not roofing contractors and do not sell repairs. Your roofer confirms what each anomaly is and what, if anything, to do about it.

Who's flying

Anyone with a drone can take a thermal picture. Reading it correctly is the job: emissivity, reflections, how long since sunset, and which anomalies are actually worth a roofer's time. That comes from training and from doing it every week.
